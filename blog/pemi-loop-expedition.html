The Pemi Loop Ultimate Daybreak Expedition

A long run in the Whites

Howard Wang … min read

Dear Mr. Fleming,

I hope you’re doing well. Being on the same team with Leilani and Matthew has been a pleasure. I’m writing to propose an exciting challenge I believe we (Leilani, Elizabeth, Aaliyah, and I) could complete together — completing the Pemi Loop in one day. We’re considering the non-fancy version of the loop, which I believe is achievable with the proper preparation and teamwork.

As outdoor enthusiasts, we seek new challenges that push us mentally and physically. The Pemi Loop, the second toughest hike in America, is one of those challenges. Completing it in a single day would be an incredible accomplishment, and I believe we can do it together. I’ve divided this proposal into key sections:

  • Why We Should Consider the Pemi Loop: I share why this hike is worth taking on, based on my experience with ultramarathons and difficult hikes like the Rim-to-Rim-to-Rim in the Grand Canyon.
  • Technical Considerations: I outline why the Pemi Loop is achievable, using my method for estimating hike times and comparing it to my past experiences.
  • How We Can Do It: I detail the route, water stops, gear, and a timeline for reaching key checkpoints to ensure we finish the hike within a day.

Why We Should Consider the Pemi Loop

As an outdoor enthusiast with experience in long-distance hiking and running, I’m always looking for new challenges. The Pemi Loop, known as the second toughest hike in America, is exactly that. Completing this hike in under one day would be an incredible accomplishment for all of us, and I believe it’s a challenge worth taking on.

I’m sharing this idea not because I consider myself the most experienced but because I know how rewarding it is to push beyond our limits. I’ve faced setbacks, several DNFs, but I’ve also completed demanding hikes such as the Rim-to-Rim-to-Rim in the Grand Canyon alongside less-experienced family members, as well as other ultramarathons. (For reference, here are some of my ultramarathon results from UltraSignup: Howard Wang — UltraSignup Results.) With our determination and preparation, I’m confident that we (Leilani, Elizabeth, and Aaliyah) can complete this hike.

Taking on the Pemi Loop in a single day will test us mentally and physically, but that’s part of the excitement. It’s an opportunity to prove what we’re capable of and to achieve something truly remarkable.

Moreover, doing the Pemi Loop offers several advantages:

  • The bragging rights of conquering a challenging trail
  • A shorter car commute compared to the Presidential Traverse
  • Less time spent in the wilderness compared to a multi-day trip

Technical Considerations (Why We Can Do It)

To gauge the difficulty and estimate time, I use a simple rule: take the Fastest Known Time (FKT) for the route and multiply it by three for normal hikers. For example, the FKT for Rim-to-Rim-to-Rim in the Grand Canyon is about 6 hours. For a good hiker, that would translate to roughly 18 hours, which matches my experience, having completed it twice with non-runners under harsh conditions around 18 to 20 hours.

Several sources, including Trekking Sketches and Reddit, suggest a reasonable time for an experienced hiker to complete the Pemi Loop is around 15–18 hours. This timeframe presents a challenging but achievable goal.

How We Can Do It

Here’s a breakdown of the Pemi Loop, including water stations and checkpoints:

  • 7.0 miLiberty Spring Tentsite (spring and tentsite 0.3 mi down Liberty Spring Trail)
  • 14.7 miGarfield Ridge Campsite (spring along the trail, with a shelter and campsites accessible via a short side trail)
  • 17.1 miGalehead Hut (potable water from a spigot and snacks available for purchase)
  • 20.7 miGuyot Shelter (spring, shelter, and campsites down a short side trail)

There are also streams for water after descending from Bondcliff. This means we only need about 1.5 liters of water and roughly 3,000 calories, such as 15 gels, at the start. Each water station can serve as a checkpoint. Based on estimates, we can plan to reach:

  • 2:20 hrsLiberty Spring Tentsite
  • 5 hrsGarfield Ridge Campsite
  • 8 hrsGalehead Hut
  • 11 hrsGuyot Shelter

We can turn back if we cannot reach the 14.7-mile mark in under 6 hours. Completing the loop in 14 to 15 hours would allow us to avoid carrying extra gear such as sleeping bags, which would slow us down. Our essential gear should include a water bladder, water filter, rain jacket, trekking poles, and enough nutrition for the day.
